# Research Overview

## Question and Contribution

How do memorial requirements become revisable lifecycle rules, and how can their
implemented consequences be inspected?

The retrospective HCI design case follows a technical maker's collaboration with
a bereaved pet owner. A broad protective proposal initially made every injury
trigger return and compulsory rest. Later correction allowed ordinary injury
and healing, reserved protected recovery for lethal events and introduced owner
release from rest. Quiet administrative removal and a separate archival route
differed in whether recovery information survived.

These revisions make three consequences visible: what an event presents, which
interaction it interrupts, and what information remains available for later
recovery. The contribution is this implemented, revisable game-case account,
with practical considerations for choosing consequences and allocating control.

## Research Status and Next Evidence

The working-paper design case supports interpretation of the documented design
decisions and inspection of their programmed consequences. The generic prototype
is later technical material from the same project.

Further work can test both implementations under one matched protocol, support
authorized clean-checkout reproduction, study owners' understanding and use
with consent, and compare human work, rework and maintenance across construction
arrangements.

The canonical build depends on an excluded, authorized private
test-infrastructure installation. The dated technical report describes actual
checks and the retained geometry failure. Reproducing the canonical build
requires that infrastructure installation.
